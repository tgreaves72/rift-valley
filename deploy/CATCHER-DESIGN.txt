RIFT STALKER | RIFT VALLEY 0.13.3

An original Temple Run-inspired pursuer built from native Roblox primitives.
Charcoal body, broad shoulder hump, jagged mane, pale angular mask, glowing green
eyes, small horns, long forearms, heavy knuckles and six claws form the silhouette.
Violet role outlines still identify catchers against the decorated maps.

APPLIES TO
Solo practice: both NPC catchers use the creature body with their existing chase AI.
Multiplayer: human catchers wear the same model over their normal avatar. The
outfit hides body parts, face decals and late-loaded accessories, then restores
their original transparency when the catcher role ends. R6/R15 root attachment
leaves the original humanoid, collisions, abilities and tag-distance checks intact.

ANIMATION
Local procedural shoulders, hips, spine and head produce a hunched loping gait.
The new authored Walk, Run and Sprint cycles use contact/passing/flight key poses.
Velocity blends locomotion into idle, with jump/fall shapes and a short landing settle.
Movement velocity blends the run into a subtle idle breathing pose.
Motor transforms animate cosmetics; server root motion still controls the NPCs.
Client skips pose updates beyond 180 studs to reduce extra work.

PREVIEW
assets/catcher-preview.png: front and three-quarter rendering from actual model geometry.
dist/RiftStalker-Design.rbxm: posed, anchored native model for design inspection.
The design preview is not a live NPC; the game constructs its live rig at runtime.
Each live creature uses 50 primitive parts with no external meshes/textures.
assets/catcher-running.gif previews the native run poses.
dist/RiftStalker-Animations.rbxm contains three looping KeyframeSequences for the
NPC skeleton. The game's shared motion sampler already plays these poses without
uploaded IDs. Import the asset into Studio to inspect/edit sequences if desired.

VALIDATION
184 checks on the actual creature modules pass, including collider/mass settings,
joint connectivity, procedural movement, skin reuse, accessory hiding and exact
avatar restoration. Native engine physics and player replication still need Studio
playtesting; use the catcher checks in deploy/DEPLOYMENT.txt.

GRAB AND CONSUME | 0.13.0
Maximum horizontal reach is 4.2 studs with a 3.2-stud vertical allowance. A
forward rectangle limits lateral reach to 3.1 and depth to 4, with 0.25 behind.
The visible arms warn for 0.75 seconds while the creature keeps pursuing. The
final 0.16 seconds commit to a short lunge capped at 3.6 studs and stop short of a
stationary victim. The server rechecks contact and wall occlusion after the windup.
Amber bruisers use the volume above; cyan stalkers have a narrower 2.3-stud
lateral half-width and 3.4-stud forward depth. One creature can threaten a runner
at a time. Missed attacks cause 1.15 seconds of recovery and runner protection.
Approved runner dashes give 0.45 seconds of server-side dodge protection, so a
late replicated position cannot overturn an accepted dodge during that window.
They immediately cancel a pending reach/lunge and trigger normal miss recovery.
The four-second dash cooldown and stamina cost remain. Sprint drain is 18 per
second, giving about 5.6 seconds from full stamina before regeneration or buffs.
Spatial warning grunts use Roblox's packaged oof.ogg at reduced pitch, with a
brief exclamation mark, outline and noncolliding contact footprint.
Charging stalkers appear in course 3: warn for 0.75 seconds, charge in a fixed
direction for 0.55 seconds, then recover. Ordinary pursuit stays slower than sprint.
Confirmed contact locks both actors for a short lift/swallow sequence. A local
whole-avatar proxy moves into the mouth and shrinks uniformly; no body parts
detach. Server cleanup restores original visibility and turns the runner into a
catcher. The victim's temporary camera and proxy also clean up on cancellation.


0.5.0 added a finite Dash action to the animation model. During approved dashes,
the creature leans forward and sweeps its arms back with curling wind ribbons.
Stalker NPCs progress from two to six per section, with a 12-bot split-group budget.
Native Studio testing must verify NPC navigation and jumping across course gaps.

BONUS-COURSE NAVIGATION | 0.13.0
Bruiser speed by course: 29/30/31/32/32.5. Walking is 22 and sprint is 34.
Stalkers in courses 3-5 use 32.25/33/33.5; a warned charge uses 41.
NPCs and human catchers start at supported stations ahead of arriving runners,
facing the entry. Initial protection starts after Ready and lasts 2.5 seconds.
All later portal landings use the same grace. NPCs pursue arrivals immediately;
the protection gates grabs and charges, rather than movement. Initial paths
prepare during Get Ready. Portal finishes reconcile the next pack immediately.
Server validation blocks attacks throughout arrival protection.
Horizontal waypoint progress accounts for the root sitting above the floor.
Grounded paths replan on blockage and exclude Unstable-labelled crumble tiles.
Charges/lunges require stable body-width ground and low-obstacle clearance.
Recovery invalidates stale routes, clears charge state and delays attacks.
Direct pursuit now discards the previous path. If support or an obstacle needs
pathfinding again, compute from the current position instead of doubling back
to old nodes. Version 0.12.0 Studio tests verified this across all three causeway
variants. The new 0.13.0 stations and interception balance await a Studio run.
